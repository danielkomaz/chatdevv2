# Phase 1: Concept & Story — World Building

## Overview

This phase establishes the creative foundation of the game. The **Game Director** and **Story Writer** collaborate to define every narrative and world-building element before any design or development begins. Nothing proceeds to Phase 2 until the quality gate at the end of this document is fully satisfied.

---

## Objective

Produce a complete, cohesive creative bible that answers:
- What is this game about?
- Who are the characters?
- What world do they inhabit?
- What is the central conflict?
- How does the story unfold across three acts?

All deliverables must be internally consistent, original, and detailed enough that any team member can reference this document without needing additional clarification.

---

## Required Outputs

### 1. Game Title
A single title. May include a subtitle separated by a colon or dash.

**Example Format:**
```
Title: Ember Crown: Age of the Shattered Throne
```

---

### 2. Elevator Pitch
Two to three sentences that communicate the genre, setting, tone, and core hook. Should be usable as a store description opener.

**Example Format:**
```
Ember Crown is a turn-based JRPG set in a fractured world where five ancient races 
struggle for survival after their god-forge was destroyed. Players lead a ragtag 
party of outcasts through treacherous dungeons and war-torn kingdoms to prevent 
a second, more catastrophic Shattering. Featuring deep tactical combat, rich lore, 
and branching character arcs, Ember Crown rewards both veterans and newcomers alike.
```

---

### 3. World Name and Lore Summary
The name of the game world and a 200–400 word summary of its history, cosmology, and current state.

**Must include:**
- World name
- Creation myth or founding age
- Major historical event(s) that shaped the current era
- Current political/social landscape
- Why the world is in conflict now

**Example Format:**
```
World Name: Aelundra

Lore Summary:
In the age before memory, the god-forge Sorveth breathed life into five peoples 
and set them upon the continent of Aelundra. For millennia the races coexisted 
uneasily, united only by their reverence for the Ember Crown — a relic said to 
hold Sorveth's final spark...
[continues to 200-400 words]
```

---

### 4. Playable Races (Minimum 5)
Each race must have: name, physical description, homeland, cultural identity, historical role, and narrative significance.

**Example Format:**
```
Race: Draconian
Physical Description: Tall, scaled humanoids with vestigial wings and slit pupils. 
  Scales range from obsidian black to deep crimson. Average height 6'4".
Homeland: The Ashspire Mountains, volcanic peaks in the northern reaches of Aelundra.
Cultural Identity: Honor-bound warrior society organized into Warclan structures. 
  Oral history transmitted through ritual combat chants.
Historical Role: Served as the god-forge's wardens for three thousand years until 
  the Shattering dissolved their sacred covenant.
Narrative Significance: The protagonist's closest ally is Draconian; the player 
  will explore themes of duty vs. freedom through this race's arc.

[Repeat for all 5+ races]
```

**Minimum races to define:** Human, Elf, Dwarf, Draconian, Sprite. Additional races encouraged.

---

### 5. Main Conflict Overview
A 150–300 word description of the central antagonistic force, what it wants, and why it cannot be ignored. Must explain the stakes if the heroes fail.

**Must include:**
- Primary antagonistic force (person, faction, or phenomenon)
- Their goal and motivation
- Why conventional powers have failed to stop them
- What happens to the world if they succeed

**Example Format:**
```
The Voidweavers, a death-cult formed from survivors of the original Shattering, 
have spent centuries reverse-engineering fragments of the god-forge. Their leader, 
the Pale Architect, believes that existence itself is a mistake and seeks to feed 
Aelundra's life-essence back into the void from which Sorveth drew it...
```

---

### 6. Protagonist Profile
Full character sheet for the main playable character (or lead character if the party is customizable).

**Must include:**
- Full name and nickname/title
- Race and age
- Background and origin story (birthplace, family, formative events)
- Personality traits (3–5 adjectives + elaboration)
- Personal motivation (what they want at story start vs. what they need)
- Character arc summary (how they change across the three acts)
- Relationships to other main characters
- Signature quote

**Example Format:**
```
Name: Kael Dawnridge (called "Ashwalker" by enemies)
Race: Human | Age: 24
Background: Born in the border town of Cinderford, Kael lost his family when 
  Voidweaver raiding parties razed the settlement during a failed census expedition...
Personality: Pragmatic, sardonic, fiercely loyal, haunted, quietly compassionate
Personal Motivation (want): Find and punish those responsible for Cinderford's 
  destruction. (need): Learn that justice and revenge are not the same thing.
Character Arc: Act 1 — lone wolf reluctantly accepting help. Act 2 — learning 
  to trust and lead. Act 3 — sacrificing personal vengeance for collective survival.
Signature Quote: "I stopped praying when the gods stopped answering. Now I just 
  show up and deal with what's in front of me."
```

---

### 7. Antagonist Profile
Full character sheet for the primary villain. Must be three-dimensional — not purely evil for its own sake.

**Must include:**
- Full name and title
- Race and age
- Background and origin story
- Core belief/worldview (what they think is true that drives their actions)
- Why they believe they are right
- The tragedy or wound that set them on their path
- Relationship to the protagonist (direct or thematic)
- Signature quote

**Example Format:**
```
Name: Seraphel the Pale Architect
Race: Half-Elf (born before the formal recognition of mixed lineage) | Age: 340+
Background: Once a senior scholar in the Covenant of Sorveth, Seraphel 
  witnessed the Shattering firsthand and concluded that the god-forge's 
  destruction was not an accident but a mercy...
Core Belief: Life without purpose is cruelty prolonged. The world has lost 
  its architect; the kindest act is to return its materials to the void.
Why They Think They're Right: Every year since the Shattering has brought 
  new suffering. No replacement for Sorveth has emerged. To Seraphel, 
  hope itself is the cruelest lie.
Wound: Lost their entire family lineage — spanning decades — to the 
  post-Shattering plague wars. Never recovered.
Relationship to Protagonist: Seraphel was present at the destruction of 
  Cinderford and considers Kael a "loose thread" — but comes to respect 
  his persistence.
Signature Quote: "I do not hate the living. I simply remember what it felt 
  like before they had to suffer."
```

---

### 8. Three-Act Story Outline

A bullet-point breakdown of the narrative across three acts. Each act must have: opening situation, key events (minimum 3 per act), midpoint/turning point, and closing situation/cliffhanger.

**Example Format:**

#### Act 1: The Gathering Storm
- Kael arrives in the city of Velmoor seeking a bounty hunter contract
- Meets Sera (Elf healer) and Gorrak (Dwarf artificer) under hostile circumstances
- First encounter with Voidweaver agents; discovers the cult is active again
- Follows a trail of cult activity to the ruins of the Covenant of Sorveth
- Discovers a fragment of the Ember Crown still radiates power
- **Act 1 Turning Point:** The fragment reacts to Kael — revealing a bloodline connection to the old Covenant wardens. The party is now hunted.

#### Act 2: The Fractured Alliance
- Party must forge alliances with at least two of the five races to stand against the Voidweavers
- Journey through the Ashspire Mountains to broker a deal with the Draconian Warclan
- Betrayal by a trusted NPC ally — cult infiltration runs deeper than suspected
- Discovery of the Pale Architect's true identity and personal history
- The party attempts a raid on the Voidweavers' primary sanctum — fails catastrophically
- **Act 2 Turning Point:** Kael learns that the Ember Crown, if reassembled, could either restore the god-forge OR seal the void permanently — destroying the crown and the bloodline connection with it.

#### Act 3: The Final Reckoning
- Race to recover the remaining crown fragments before Seraphel completes the void ritual
- Each party member faces a personal crisis tied to their racial/cultural arc
- Final assault on the Pale Architect's citadel built atop the original god-forge site
- Kael confronts Seraphel — discovers they share a grief that could have led to the same path
- Final choice: reassemble the crown (restore the world at unknown cost) or shatter it (seal the void, end the bloodline connection forever)
- **Ending:** Bittersweet. The void is sealed. The god-forge does not return. But the five races, for the first time, stand together without a deity to unite them — only the choices they made.

### 9. Setting Descriptions

#### Towns (Minimum 3)

Each town must include: name, location on world map, population estimate, dominant race(s), economic base, notable buildings/NPCs, atmosphere description, and gameplay role.

**Example Format:**
```
Town: Velmoor — The Crossroads City
Location: Central plains, intersection of three major trade roads
Population: ~40,000 (mixed race, majority Human and Elf)
Dominant Races: Human, Elf
Economic Base: Trade brokering, mercenary contracts, alchemical goods
Notable Buildings:
  - The Iron Register (mercenary guild, main quest hub in Act 1)
  - Silverthread Market (primary shop for Tiers 1–2 equipment)
  - The Tarnished Chalice (inn and rumor source)
  - Covenant Archive Branch (lore library, unlockable content)
Notable NPCs:
  - Magistra Ondine (guild master, quest giver)
  - Petch (info broker, comic relief)
Atmosphere: Loud, bustling, morally ambiguous. Smells of spice and coal smoke. 
  The kind of city where anything can be bought and everyone has a secret.
Gameplay Role: Primary hub for Acts 1–2. Save point, shop, party recruitment.
```

**Required Towns:** Velmoor (or equivalent major hub), a small frontier/border town, a racial capital (for at least one of the five races).

---

#### Dungeons (Minimum 3)

Each dungeon must include: name, location, lore reason for existing, tier/difficulty, key environmental hazards, boss encounter, unique mechanic, and notable loot.

**Example Format:**
```
Dungeon: The Sunken Covenant
Location: Beneath the ruins east of Velmoor, flooded lower levels
Lore: The original Covenant of Sorveth built their primary research vaults here. 
  After the Shattering, the vaults partially sank into an underground aquifer 
  and were sealed. Cult agents have been excavating the site for crown fragments.
Tier/Difficulty: Tier 1 (Acts 1–2 entry point), enemy levels 5–12
Environmental Hazards: Rising water sections (timed movement), unstable floors 
  (weight-triggered collapses), dark corridors (torchlight mechanic reduces visibility)
Boss: Archivist Mourn — once a Covenant scholar, now a lich-adjacent entity 
  sustained by residual god-forge energy. Weak to Holy. Uses debuff stacking.
Unique Mechanic: Ancient valve puzzles control water level throughout dungeon, 
  opening or flooding different paths. Some items only accessible at specific 
  water levels.
Notable Loot: Covenant Sigil Ring (accessory, +10% MP), Fragment of Remembrance 
  (key item, main quest), Scholar's Grimoire (unlocks Archivist class skill branch)
```

---

#### World Map Regions (Minimum 3)

Each region must include: name, geography, climate, dominant inhabitants, points of interest, narrative role, and travel hazard.

**Example Format:**
```
Region: The Ashspire Mountains
Geography: Jagged volcanic range running north-south along Aelundra's spine. 
  Peaks average 3,000 meters. Active geothermal activity throughout.
Climate: Dry and intensely hot in lower elevations; ash-choked and cold at altitude.
Dominant Inhabitants: Draconian Warclans, fire elementals (wild), Ashspire 
  rock lizards (passive fauna), Scorchling enemies (aggressive low-level mobs)
Points of Interest:
  - Vaultpeak Citadel (Draconian capital, major Act 2 hub)
  - The Ember Vents (natural dungeon, lava caverns)
  - The Ashwing Shrine (optional area, Draconian lore, high-tier loot)
Narrative Role: Act 2 alliance arc. Players must earn Draconian trust to 
  access Vaultpeak Citadel as a safe zone and recruit Draconian party member.
Travel Hazard: Ash storms reduce visibility and deal passive Fire damage per 
  step without fire-resist equipment. Flying enemies are common.
```

## Collaboration Workflow

### Roles

| Role | Responsibilities |
|---|---|
| **Game Director** | Sets creative vision, approves all deliverables, resolves conflicts between Story Writer output and design requirements, maintains tone consistency |
| **Story Writer** | Produces first drafts of all deliverables, develops lore in depth, maintains internal consistency, integrates feedback |

### Process

1. **Kickoff Session:** Game Director shares initial genre/tone brief with Story Writer (1–2 paragraphs maximum). Story Writer acknowledges and asks clarifying questions before drafting.
2. **Draft 1:** Story Writer produces first drafts of items 1–5 (Title, Pitch, World, Races, Conflict). Game Director reviews and leaves inline comments.
3. **Revision 1:** Story Writer incorporates feedback. If disagreement exists, Game Director has final say.
4. **Draft 2:** Story Writer produces items 6–9 (characters, story outline, settings). Game Director reviews.
5. **Revision 2:** Incorporate feedback.
6. **Full Document Review:** Game Director reads the completed document as a single cohesive whole and checks for internal consistency.
7. **Sign-off:** Game Director signs the quality gate below.

### Communication Standards
- All creative disputes are resolved by the Game Director.
- Story Writer flags any element that may create gameplay conflicts (e.g., a narrative element that is difficult to implement in a JRPG format).
- No element is marked final until it appears in the approved version of this document.
